I started writing here for a selfish reason: I don't understand something until I can explain it in order, without skipping steps.

Reading a chapter about virtual memory feels like understanding. Trying to write the explanation shows exactly where the understanding stops. The gaps show up as sentences that won't come out, or as a paragraph that quietly hand-waves over the hard part.

## Two kinds of pages

This site has two kinds of writing.

**Posts** are finished. Each one answers a single question, from the ground up, and I'm happy for it to be judged.

**Notes** are not finished, and that is the point. They are closer to a lab notebook: short, linked to each other, and rewritten whenever I learn something that breaks them. Each note says how mature it is. A seedling is a rough sketch, a growing note is taking shape, and an evergreen note is something I would defend in a review.

## What you'll find

Mostly the layer under the code: how a program actually runs, the memory hierarchy, concurrency, databases and the networks between machines. Things I am learning properly, one question at a time.
